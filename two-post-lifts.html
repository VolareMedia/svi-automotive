A two-post lift is the workhorse of most shops. The vehicle rides on four arms at its factory lift points, so the wheels hang free and the whole underside is open: brakes, exhaust, suspension, transmissions, all within reach.

Overhead models run a cross-beam across the top of the columns, which keeps the floor between the posts completely clear. Baseplate models (also called bottom-plate) route their cables and hydraulics across a low plate on the floor instead, so they fit garages where an overhead beam won't clear the ceiling.

Not sure which fits your space? We measure ceiling height, check your slab and confirm your power before you buy. Compare 2-post vs 4-post lifts.

Choose overhead ifYou have the ceiling height for the overhead beam and want a clear, flat floor between the columns.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Overhead or baseplate: which 2-post lift do I need?

It mostly comes down to ceiling height. Overhead lifts need more headroom for the top beam but leave the floor completely clear. Baseplate lifts have no overhead beam, so they fit lower ceilings. The Atlas BP8000 installs under ceilings as low as 9' 3". We measure before you buy.

What power does a 2-post lift need?

The two-post lifts we carry run on 220V single-phase power with a 30A breaker. We confirm your electrical supply before install.

Are your 2-post lifts ALI certified?

The Atlas Platinum PVL series is ALI certified and carries a 5-year structural warranty. Check each lift's page for its certification and full warranty.

Do you install 2-post lifts?

Yes. We anchor, level and load-test every lift we install, and we service and inspect lifts across Vancouver Island.
